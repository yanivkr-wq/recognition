Concept 06 — Reco design deck · SwipeDrinks-inspired

Get your tokens.
This is Swipe.

Modern fintech-grade minimalism. One signature accent (lime green). Pastel category tiles with black emblems. Selective bold typography. Big numbers with small pill annotations. The energy of a sophisticated young-adult app, scaled down for a kid who's old enough to want something that doesn't look like a kid app.

One single accent: lime. Black for everything important. Cream off-white for app background, white for cards. Pastel tiles for category emblems — 6 hues that rotate through the badge grid. No gradients. No shadows beyond a hairline. The whole concept is about restraint.

Iconography

Black silhouette icons. Sometimes inside a circle, sometimes loose on a pastel tile. No outlines, no two-tone, no gradient. The aesthetic equivalent of black ink on colored paper. Token count uses a tiny cup icon (here a coin icon) inside a small white pill — the count is the data, the icon is the texture.

Lime is reserved exclusively for the primary CTA (the central "Scan/Submit" tab button, the streak chip, the highlighted active row in the ledger).

— concept 06 notes —

Strongest weakness

This is the most "grown-up" concept of the six — and that's exactly its risk. The SwipeDrinks design language was built for young adults at festivals; lifting it to a 9–11 year old means the app reads as cool but possibly cold. The aesthetic has very low chrome — no characters, no mascot, no playful flourishes. For a kid who thinks "kid apps" are embarrassing, this is exactly what she wants. For a kid who needs warmth and encouragement, this might feel like an iPhone settings screen. Run a 30-second test on Lia and Yael with the home screen of this concept versus Plush — that one taste will tell you which side of the line they're on.

Quietest strength: this concept's restraint scales. Spark and Plush bet hard on illustrations and mascots — both demand ongoing design investment to look right. Swipe bets on typography, layout, and a single accent color. That's cheap to maintain and ages well: the kids' tablet shows the same app in three years that it shows today, just with bigger numbers. The "Scan" tab metaphor also gives the photo-evidence feature a confident, gestural identity that none of the other concepts have.
