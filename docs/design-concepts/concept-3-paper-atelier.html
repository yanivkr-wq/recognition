Paper
Atelier

Reco as a private studio notebook. Warm cream paper, terracotta and dusty teal, washi tape, hand-stamped doodles, soft sticker shadows. The non-babyish version of "cozy" — closer to a Muji notebook than a Crayola activity book. Built to age with the kid: still works at 13.

— concept 03 notes —

Strongest weakness

The atelier aesthetic is the calmest of the three — and that's exactly its weakness. Kids at 9–11 are at peak "I want my app to feel cool, not cozy." Soft cream paper and handwritten margins risk reading as a parent's idea of what a kid app should look like rather than something the kid would proudly show a friend. The competitive leaderboards that Lily explicitly wanted to surface (sibling visibility) sit uneasily in this register — terracotta and teal are friendly colors, not "I won" colors.

Quietest strength: this is the only concept whose visual personality survives tonal shifts. The Hero Dossier needs ceremony at all times; the Arcade Neon needs energy. The Atelier reads the same when the app delivers "campaign complete!" celebration as when it delivers "you used a streak freeze" gentleness. That tonal range matters in a kid-facing product that has to handle wins, losses, and waiting all in the same week — and it's also the easiest of the three to evolve as the kids age into 13 and 15.
